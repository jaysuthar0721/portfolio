Active Fin Stabilization Rocket

Long Shot was Ram Rocketry's attempt to control a rocket's attitude with moving fins. It used four servo-driven fins above a set of fixed stabilizing fins. We wanted to see whether the active fins could produce a measurable roll response during coast without compromising stability during motor burn.

My focus: Project leadership, system architecture, aerodynamic analysis, flight simulation, controls development, and integration.

Vehicle & electronics

The rocket was about five feet long, three inches in diameter, and 2.2 kg. An AeroTech H100W motor was expected to take it to roughly 130 m/s. Its control system used a BNO055 IMU, an LPS35HW barometer, a custom flight-control board, and four high-torque servos.

Flight dynamics & controls

I built a six-degree-of-freedom MATLAB/Simulink model to study the rocket's motion, including changing mass during the burn, drag, fin forces, descent, and flight-to-flight variation. We kept the active fins locked during powered ascent, then commanded positive, negative, and neutral deflections during coast so we could compare the response.

Aerodynamic analysis

I examined several fin deflections in SolidWorks Flow Simulation at velocities up to 130 m/s before manufacturing. I looked at pressure distribution, wake behavior, tip vortices, and how changes to the trailing edge affected the flow.

Project integration

The project forced us to connect the mechanical design, control software, sensors, and launch operations. Our second version flew without the oscillation seen earlier and stabilized during flight. The work also helped establish the approach we later used for active apogee control on Bighorn.
