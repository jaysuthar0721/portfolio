Project 01 / MECH 307 / Spring 2026 / Role: Electronics Architecture & Flight Controls

Variable Geometry Drone

[ Fig 01_A: Assembled drone on constrained attitude test rig ]
Background

Our MECH 307 team set out to build a quadcopter with secondary arms that rotate through a gear-and-servo mechanism. Within one semester, we needed to integrate the mechanism, electronics, and a custom flight controller, then demonstrate attitude control on a constrained test stand. We had no off-the-shelf flight computer or vendor documentation for the chosen electronic speed controllers (ESCs).

My work

I was responsible for the electronics architecture and attitude control. I used two ESP32 boards with ESP-NOW for wireless commands and an MPU-6050 motion sensor for feedback. A complementary filter and a 44 Hz digital low-pass filter helped reduce the effect of motor vibration on the measurements.

When the ESCs did not respond to standard servo PWM, I switched the motor commands to DSHOT. I also traced damage on an earlier hardware revision to an undocumented Vbat connection in the controller pinout. That changed how we checked connections before powering the system.

I tuned the controller on a two-axis test stand. At the gains we tested, the integral term made the response too aggressive, so I removed it and continued with proportional and derivative control.

Results

The drone demonstrated approximately one second of self-stabilized attitude control on the constrained test stand. During that interval, the controller used motion-sensor feedback to generate corrective motor commands. This was a brief test-stand result, not sustained free flight.

Wireless commands and sensor feedback worked reliably during testing. I documented the connector issues, wiring lessons, and time needed for firmware revisions so future MECH 307 teams could build on the work.
