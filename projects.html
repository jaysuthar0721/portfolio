Project 01 / MECH 307 / Spring 2026 / Role: Electronics Architecture & Flight Controls

Variable Geometry Drone

[ Fig 01_A: Assembled drone on constrained attitude test rig ]
Challenge

Design and demonstrate a quadcopter whose secondary arms rotate in flight through an integrated gear and servo actuation system, and prove closed-loop attitude control on a constrained test stand within a single semester. The team had to integrate a mechanical actuation mechanism, a custom electronics stack, and a flight controller without the benefit of an off-the-shelf flight computer or vendor documentation for the chosen ESCs.

Action

I owned the electronics architecture and the PID controls path. I selected and integrated a dual-ESP32 transmit and receive system using ESP-NOW for low-latency manual control, paired with an MPU-6050 IMU running through a complementary filter and a 44 Hz digital low-pass to reject motor vibration. When the brushless ESCs would not respond to standard servo PWM, I rebuilt the motor command layer around the DSHOT protocol and isolated an undocumented Vbat connection on the ESC pinout that had damaged a previous hardware revision, which became a permanent wire-management rule for the team. I tuned the PID loops empirically on a 2-axis test stand and dropped the integral term once it proved more aggressive than useful at our gain levels, running stable P and D control instead.

Impact

Demonstrated approximately one second of self-stabilized closed-loop attitude control on the test stand, with the controller correctly generating corrective motor commands from IMU feedback in real time. Wireless control and IMU pipeline performed reliably across all flight tests, with manual control inputs received and acted on correctly throughout testing. Produced a written lessons-learned record covering connector specification, wire management, and realistic firmware iteration budgets, which is being handed forward to future MECH 307 teams.
